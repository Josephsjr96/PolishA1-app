💘 Flirting in Poland

Real lines people actually say — plus the part most phrasebooks skip: what the answer means. Polish "no" is usually polite and indirect, so it is easy to miss on purpose.

Rule zero: the first no is the last word. Charm is fine, persistence is not — and unwanted pursuit after a refusal (nękanie) is a criminal offence here, not a romantic-comedy move.

🤬 Street Talk & Swearing

This tab is for understanding, not for speaking. It is here so you know whether someone is angry at the world, joking with you, or actually insulting you — and so you can tell the difference before you react.

A foreigner swearing in Polish lands very differently than a local doing it. Recognise everything, say almost none of it. Public insults (znieważenie) and swearing at police carry real fines here.

1 The sounds cz, sz, rz, dz

Polish uses letter combinations for single sounds. They're not two separate letters.

ComboSoundEnglish hint
czchchurch
szshshoe
rz / żzhmeasure
dzdzadze
dzijjeans
Cześć · Przepraszam · Dziękuję All contain these combos

2 The letter c

Polish c is never "k". It sounds like ts in "cats".

Dobranoc → dob-RAH-nots Not "dobranok"

3 The letter ł

Polish ł sounds like English w.

Miło mi → MEE-woh mee Not "meelo"

4 Nasal vowels ą and ę

These are nasal — air goes through your nose. Similar to French on and in.

LetterSounds likeExample
ą"on"są
ę"en"język
Pięć minut → pyench MEE-noot Air through the nose, don't close with "n"

5 Stress rule

Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable in almost every Polish word.

koBIEta · DObrze · dzień DObry Exceptions: short words often stress first syllable

6 Greetings — formal vs informal

Polish has a strong formal/informal distinction.

InformalFormalMeaning
CześćDzień dobryHello
Na razieDo widzeniaBye
Jak się masz?Jak się pan/pani ma?How are you?

7 pan / pani (formal "you")

In formal Polish, "you" becomes pan (to a man) or pani (to a woman), used in 3rd person form.

Jak się pan nazywa? What is your name? (to a man)

8 The word się

się is a reflexive particle. It often means "oneself" or is part of a verb phrase.

Jak się masz? · Nazywam się Anna. How are you? · I am called Anna.

9 "A ty?" — And you?

a = "and / but", ty = "you" (informal singular).

Dobrze, a ty? Good, and you?

10 The verb być (to be)

The most important Polish verb. Learn it by heart.

PersonPolishEnglish
jajestemI am
tyjesteśyou are
on / onajesthe / she is
myjesteśmywe are
wyjesteścieyou are (pl.)
oni / onesąthey are

11 "Mam na imię…"

Literally "I have for name…". Polish doesn't say "My name is" — it uses this pattern.

Mam na imię Anna. My name is Anna. (lit. I have for name Anna)

12 Accusative case (biernik)

Used for the direct object of a sentence — what you want, have, or order. Feminine nouns ending in -a change to -ę.

NominativeAccusativeEnglish
kawakawęcoffee
herbataherbatętea
AnnaAnnęAnna
Poproszę kawę. A coffee, please. (kawa → kawę)

13 Adjectives in accusative

Adjectives describing feminine nouns also change to -ą in accusative.

NominativeAccusativeEnglish
dużadużąlarge
małamałąsmall
dobradobrągood
Dużą czy małą? Large or small? (about coffee — feminine)

14 "Poproszę…" — polite ordering

Poproszę = "I'd like / I ask for". The polite way to order anything.

Poproszę kawę. · Poproszę menu. A coffee, please. · Menu, please.

15 "Ile to kosztuje?" — How much?

Essential shopping phrase.

20 Yes/No questions

Polish doesn't need a helper verb. Just change intonation, or start with czy.

Daleko? · Czy to daleko? Is it far? · Is it far? (more formal)

22 Genitive case (dopełniacz)

Used after "of", with quantities, and in many set phrases like Miłego dnia.

Miłego dnia! · Pięć minut · dużo pracy Have a nice day! · Five minutes · a lot of work

Miłego dnia is genitive — literally "of a nice day".

23 "z" + genitive = from

z means "from" and takes the genitive case.

Nominativez + Genitive
Polskaz Polski
Angliaz Anglii
Niemcyz Niemiec
Jestem z Polski. I am from Poland.

This is the number-one giveaway when a foreigner flirts. Adjectives agree with the person being described, and past-tense verbs agree with whoever is speaking.

27 Pan / Pani → ty

Strangers get Pan (sir) / Pani (madam) with the verb in the third person. Nobody flirts in that form — you first have to move to ty, and there is a fixed sentence for it.

FormalInformalEnglish
Czy Pani ma ochotę…Masz ochotę…Do you fancy…
Jak się Pan nazywa?Jak masz na imię?What's your name?
Przepraszam PaniąPrzepraszam cięSorry
Możemy przejść na ty? Can we switch to "ty"? — ask it, don't assume it

28 Calling someone by name (wołacz)

Polish has a special case just for addressing people. Using the plain name where the vocative belongs sounds cold — using the warm diminutive is half of Polish affection.

NameCalling themFeel
AnnaAnno! / Aniu!formal / warm
KatarzynaKasiu!warm
TomekTomku!normal
Pan / PaniProszę Pana / Panipolite
Aniu, masz ochotę na kawę? Ania, do you fancy a coffee?

29 podobać się · lubić · kochać

Three verbs, three completely different messages. Mixing them up either friend-zones you or terrifies the other person.

PolishEnglishStrength
Podobasz mi się.I'm attracted to you.romantic opener
Lubię cię.I like you (as a person).friendly only
Kocham cię.I love you.heavy — months in

Note the structure: podobać się flips the sentence — literally "you are pleasing to me" (mi = to me, ci = to you).

Podoba mi się twój styl. Your style is pleasing to me → I like your style.

30 Why one word is everywhere

Kurwa is the hinge of spoken Polish. Literally it is a very rude word for a prostitute, but in ordinary speech it works as a comma, an exclamation mark and a pause-filler.

Heard asMeansAimed at you?
Kurwa, gdzie klucze?Damn, where are the keys?no
Ja pierdolę!Holy s***! (surprise)no
Ty kurwo!You b****!yes

The test is simple: no "ty" and no name = not about you. Someone swearing at a broken ticket machine is not starting a fight with you.

31 Recognising an order aimed at you

Polish commands (imperatives) are short and end abruptly. If you hear one of these shapes, someone is telling you to do something — that is the moment to disengage, not to reply.

FormExampleEnglish
-ajSpadaj!Get lost!
-aj sięOdwal się!Back off!
-ij / -yjZamknij się!Shut up!
bare nounWypad!Out!
ty + nounTy debilu!You moron!
Nie chcę problemów. Już idę. I don't want trouble. I'm leaving. — the only answer worth giving
